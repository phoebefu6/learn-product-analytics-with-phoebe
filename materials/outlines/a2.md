"learn-product-analytics-with-phoebe / Leader session 2 of 6" · The moment that predicts staying. Level 🟡 Core, audience PMs and product leaders, no code.
Part 1 · covers activation vs the aha moment: definitions (activation = a measurable early behaviour chosen because it predicts later return; aha = the user's felt moment of value; the metric is a proxy for the feeling). Figure: a cohort splitting at an early behaviour into two streams with two later-return rates (use bench canon: 1,186 came back on a second day, 13.0% vs 2.4% for the 7,162 others). Cards: "Activation is a definition you choose" (live, open), "Aha is felt, activation is counted" (self).
Part 2 · covers the folklore: Facebook 7 friends in 10 days (REPORTED: transcript of Chamath Palihapitiya's Oct 2012 talk "How we put Facebook on the path to 1 billion users", quoted by Startup Archive: "The single biggest thing we realized was to get any individual to 7 friends in 10 days"; video not watched; Benn Stancil, Mode, 30 Jan 2015: a memorable rally cry; Mixpanel blog reports Andrew Chen saying it could as easily have been 10 in 12 or 5 in 1). Slack 2,000 messages (READ: Butterfield, First Round Review, 27 Feb 2015: "any team that has exchanged 2,000 messages in its history has tried Slack - really tried it"; "after 2,000 messages, 93% of those customers are still using Slack today"; "Based on experience of which companies stuck with us and which didn't"). Teach the disagreement: rally cry vs evidence; the survivorship problem (a team must already stay a while to send 2,000). Figure: timeline genre, two claims, badge tiers reported vs read-at-source, with what each can and cannot prove. Real world example: a team copying "7 friends" into its own product.
Part 3 · covers predictor vs lever: bench canon (push on day one 1.74x on 33.3%; came back on a second day 5.44x on 14.2%; 3+ events 2.93x on 46.0%; busy birth hour 0.88x). Strong predictors are often early versions of the outcome; causation needs an experiment (one-line link to experimentation course). Figure: before-after "reading a lift" wrong way (it causes staying) vs right way (it predicts, test it).
Build-along (no code): "Your activation hypothesis sheet": pick three candidates from their a1 tracking plan, write each as "at least k X within N days", predict share activated and lift, write the experiment that would test causation. Figure: the one-page hypothesis sheet sketch (constructed example row).
