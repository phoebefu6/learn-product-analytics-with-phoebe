Product Analytics: Events to Decisions

A product only knows what it wrote down. Every argument about activation, engagement or an AI feature comes back to the same few questions: which event, counted how, for which people, from which start. Six leader sessions teach those questions without code, from the tracking plan to the product review. Eight analyst sessions answer them in Python on real public GitHub activity from GH Archive, and end on a bench where eight activation definitions are tested on 8,348 real new repositories, live in your browser. Coming back on a second day in week one predicts later return at 5.44x. A busy first hour predicts nothing at all.

What is real here 🧾

A course about honest product numbers should be exact about which of its own numbers are measured.

ClaimStatus
The event streamReal. GH Archive's hourly files of public GitHub activity, streamed directly: one full hour for the analyst build-alongs, 252 sampled hours for the bench, 56 for the engagement curves
The bench cohortReal. 8,348 public repositories born between 15:00 and 15:59 UTC on 4 March 2024, one per account, bot accounts excluded, followed every second hour through week one and through days 28 to 41
Every lift, rate and count on the benchComputed in your browser from those rows; an independent Python run agrees on every count
New repositories, not new peopleA deliberate choice. The public stream never records a signup, so "first seen" would mostly find old accounts; a repository's birth is recorded exactly once
The engagement curvesReal, and sampled: a fixed 5 percent of accounts, seen in two hours a day for 28 days. The shape says as much about the sampling as about GitHub, and the pages say so
The code-assistant event streamConstructed from a fixed seed. No public AI-feature event stream exists; every page and chart that uses it says so
Facebook's 7 friends in 10 daysReported only, through transcripts of a 2012 talk; taught as contested folklore, never as law
Slack's 2,000 messages, and Ziegler and colleagues on acceptance rateRead at the source: the 2015 interview and the 2022 paper
